Project Motivation & Architecture Overview

Global electronic waste generation exceeds 62 million metric tons annually, yet documented formal recycling rates hover below 23%. In extended producer responsibility (EPR) reporting and corporate sustainability disclosures, an institutional failure mode routinely recurs: collection is conflated with verified processing. Drop-off receipts and aggregation logs are booked as proof of material recovery, enabling premature carbon offsets and ESG claims while equipment is in transit, stockpiled, downcycled, or lost to informal overseas processing.

LoopProof addresses this verification gap by treating e-waste tracking as a strict, evidence-gated finite state machine. Rather than accepting vendor self-certifications or claiming that blockchain magically guarantees physical recycling, LoopProof enforces cryptographic accountability: each lifecycle transition requires assigned role authorization, non-reusable SHA-256 evidence-file integrity checking, and physical mass-balance validation (total reported output mass ≤ recorded intake mass).

Crucially, numerical environmental impact metrics remain completely unavailable until an independent verifier accepts the claim. The live application demonstrates this workflow using Supabase Auth and PostgreSQL Row Level Security, alongside a reference smart contract suite tested locally on Hardhat.

1. The E-Waste Verification Gap Context & Failure Modes
The Conflation of Intake with Outcome

In conventional e-waste supply chains, certificates of recycling are commonly issued upon initial drop-off at municipal centers or corporate buyback facilities. Aggregators record intake weight, issue a collection manifest, and enterprise customers immediately calculate avoided emissions in corporate ESG disclosures.

This creates a dangerous moral hazard. Between collection and genuine mechanical or chemical refining lies a complex, multi-tiered broker network. Batches are frequently stockpiled, abandoned during commodity price drops, shredded without toxic fraction capture, or exported to developing regions under the guise of "second-hand electronics." Collection demonstrates logistics; it does not demonstrate recycling.

The Oracle Problem in Physical Supply Chains

Many enterprise blockchain proposals claim to solve supply chain opacity by stamping receipts onto a ledger. However, they frequently succumb to the supply chain oracle problem: asserting that recording data on a distributed ledger somehow guarantees physical truth in the real world.

A blockchain ledger cannot inspect a chemical leaching bath, verify furnace operating temperatures, or detect counterfeit lab assay sheets. Claiming that "blockchain proves recycling happened" is factually false and undermines engineering integrity. Software can only guarantee what was recorded, by whom, at what time, and whether the data obeyed immutable physical and role-based constraints.

2. The LoopProof Approach: Cryptographic Accountability Design Principles

LoopProof departs from speculative automation. Instead of asserting that software guarantees physical reality, LoopProof models evidence-anchored accountability. We define exactly what the ledger proves—and explicitly disclose what it does not prove.

The Three Foundational Invariants of LoopProof

1. Gated Environmental Impact (D-08): No carbon offset, landfill diversion tonnage, or ESG impact number is calculated, displayed in the UI, or exported via API until a batch reaches the VERIFIED terminal state. Unverified batches display explicit "Impact Unavailable" notices.

2. Enforced Independence (D-15, D-17): The verifier must be different from the assigned collector and processor. Self-verification or circular reviews trigger hard reverts.

3. Mass-Balance Conservation (D-05): The physical law of mass conservation is enforced at integer gram precision: total reported output mass ≤ recorded intake mass across all claimed fractions.

Human-in-the-Loop AI Classification (D-06, D-07)

Computer vision or LLM-based device classification models are treated strictly as advisory suggestions. A human registrar must review and confirm the device category code. No AI-controlled key holds any lifecycle role, and AI cannot approve batches or verify recycling claims.

Zero Tokenomics & Anti-Greenwashing (D-09)

LoopProof deliberately excludes speculative tokens, NFTs, carbon credit minting, and DAOs. By eliminating secondary financialization, the system removes perverse incentives to generate fictitious recycling records for token rewards.

The Truth vs. Trust Spectrum in Digital Product Passports

A cryptographic hash functions as an unforgeable digital notary stamp: it proves that an uploaded bill of lading, weighbridge ticket, or laboratory assay has not been modified by a single bit since anchoring. It does not certify that the physical scale was calibrated or that the operator was truthful. LoopProof addresses this reality by pairing immutable SHA-256 evidence-file integrity checking with institutional separation of duties, role-based liability, and mathematical mass-balance limits.

Physical Material Stream Complexities & Basel Convention Compliance
Printed Circuit Boards (PCBs):

High precious metal concentration (gold, copper, palladium) combined with hazardous brominated flame retardants (BFRs) and lead soldering. Without output mass-balance gating, valuable metals are stripped while toxic residues are dumped or informally burned.

IT & Telecom Hardware (Servers, Laptops):

Prone to illicit transboundary shipment under fraudulent "second-hand reuse donation" manifests, bypassing Basel Convention hazardous waste transport restrictions. LoopProof binds assigned facilities directly to certified custody logs.

3. Evidence-Gated Lifecycle State Machine Specification & Rules

Every e-waste batch in LoopProof progresses through an explicit, non-bypassable finite state machine. Transitions are executed through role-gated smart contract functions and PostgreSQL SECURITY DEFINER procedures. Arbitrary state regression is mathematically impossible; the only backward branch is a structured audit return requiring fresh evidence.

Figure 1: LoopProof State Machine Architecture with Role Assignments, Transition Gates, and Audit Feedback Loop.
Exhaustive State Machine Transition Specification
From State Function (Action) To State Authorized Actor Required Payload & Invariants
NONE registerBatch REGISTERED REGISTRAR_ROLE Unique claim key, category, collector, processor, verifier (distinct).
REGISTERED recordCollection COLLECTED Assigned Collector Intake mass > 0 (integer grams), new SHA-256 evidence hash.
REGISTERED cancelBatch CANCELLED Assigned Registrar Pre-collection window only. Terminal state.
COLLECTED recordReceipt RECEIVED Assigned Processor Facility intake confirmation, new SHA-256 evidence hash.
RECEIVED submitProcessing PROCESSED_CLAIMED Assigned Processor 1–16 material fractions, total output mass ≤ intake mass, new SHA-256 hash.
PROCESSED_CLAIMED verifyBatch VERIFIED Assigned Verifier Checklist confirmation. Unlocks impact numbers. Terminal.
PROCESSED_CLAIMED returnBatch RETURNED Assigned Verifier Mandatory ReturnReason code (1..4). Non-terminal.
PROCESSED_CLAIMED rejectBatch REJECTED Assigned Verifier Mandatory RejectReason code (1..4). Terminal.
RETURNED resubmitProcessing PROCESSED_CLAIMED Assigned Processor Replaces output set, requires genuinely fresh SHA-256 evidence hash.
Revision History Tracking (DECISIONS D-13)

When a verifier returns a claim, the processor cannot erase the past. Resubmission replaces the active output set for review, but permanently preserves previous revisions (historical evidence hashes, timestamps, reported output masses, and return reasons) in an append-only audit trail queryable via getRevision().

Global Evidence Hash Uniqueness (DECISIONS D-04)

The contract maintains a global evidence mapping: reusing a previously anchored SHA-256 digest—within the same batch or across any other batch in the system—reverts immediately. Resubmitting after a return requires genuinely fresh primary documentation.

Explicit Typed Reason Codes (DECISIONS D-14)
ReturnReason (Non-Terminal)
  • INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE (1): Incomplete stage records.
  • EVIDENCE_UNREADABLE (2): Corrupted file or hash mismatch.
  • OUTPUT_DATA_INCOMPLETE (3): Missing fractions/materials.
  • MASS_DISCREPANCY (4): Unexplained mass drop vs intake.
RejectReason (Terminal)
  • EVIDENCE_INCONSISTENT (1): Contradictory assay data.
  • MASS_BALANCE_IMPLAUSIBLE (2): Unrealistic recovery yields.
  • PROCESSOR_NOT_QUALIFIED (3): Facility uncertified for stream.
  • CHAIN_OF_CUSTODY_BROKEN (4): Missing transit custody handover.

4. Dual Implementation Architecture Full-Stack Design

LoopProof implements a dual-track architecture: a live web application backed by Supabase Auth and PostgreSQL Row Level Security for interactive evaluation, alongside a Solidity smart contract reference specification verified locally in Hardhat. The live application records actions in Supabase PostgreSQL; smart contracts are currently tested locally and are not deployed to a public blockchain.

Figure 2: LoopProof Dual Architecture — Deployed Supabase Cloud Application & Solidity Smart Contract Reference Engine.
Database Architecture & Privilege Separation
Table / Object Access Control Policy Data Structure & Immutability Guarantee
public.batches Authenticated SELECT only. Direct INSERT/UPDATE revoked. Batch state, intake mass, locked assignees (collector, processor, verifier), timestamps.
public.batch_history Authenticated SELECT only. Append-only via trigger. State transition logs: (from_state, to_state, actor_id, evidence_hash, timestamp).
public.evidence_records Authenticated SELECT only. Mutation revoked. SHA-256 digest, storage path, MIME type, submitter role. Unique hash index.
public.batch_outputs Authenticated SELECT only. Replaced per revision. Output fractions (material code + mass in grams). Sum bounded by intake mass.
public.batch_revisions Authenticated SELECT only. Append-only. Snapshots of processing claims, historical hashes, and verifier return reason codes.
Security Definer RPC Execution Model

Direct write operations from web clients are blocked. All state transitions execute through transactional PostgreSQL SECURITY DEFINER functions (lp_register_batch, lp_record_collection, lp_record_receipt, lp_submit_processing, lp_verify_batch, lp_return_batch, lp_reject_batch, lp_cancel_batch). Each procedure enforces an explicit empty search path (SET search_path = '') to eliminate search path injection vulnerabilities.

Privacy by Design & Zero-PII Policy

Supply chain transparency must not compromise enterprise confidentiality or donor privacy. Batches are indexed by a 32-byte salted hash commitment (bytes32). Underlying device serial numbers, IMEI tags, and customer identities remain off-chain. The LoopProofRegistry.sol ABI accepts zero string or dynamic bytes parameters, preventing accidental PII leakage on-chain.

6. Transparent Limitations & Disclosures Honest Scope

1. Synthetic Prototype Data: The 7 demo batches on the public dashboard (web/lib/demo-data.ts) are synthetic mock records created for UI demonstration. They do not represent real-world physical devices, recycling facilities, or actual laboratory assays.

2. Evidence-File Integrity vs. Physical Truth: An anchored SHA-256 digest checks evidence-file integrity—confirming that the digital document has not been altered since registration. It does not prove that the document was truthful when written, nor that physical recycling took place.

3. Local Hardhat Execution Status: Solidity contracts have been thoroughly tested on a simulated local Hardhat network (EVM chainId 31337). They are not deployed to any public testnet or mainnet. The live app records actions in Supabase, not on-chain.

4. Absence of Security Audit: The codebase is an academic prototype developed for the hackathon. It has not undergone an independent professional third-party smart contract security audit.

Phase 2: Multi-Party Verifier Quorum

Transition from single-verifier assignments to a decentralized verification pool. Implement threshold BLS signatures requiring k-of-n independent accredited auditors to sign off on high-volume or hazardous material streams.

Phase 3: Automated IoT Scale Integration

Connect certified industrial weighing platforms directly to registrar and collector modules. Use cryptographic hardware security modules (HSMs) on weighbridges to sign gross-tare-net tickets directly at the facility gate.

Phase 4: Downstream Material Passports

Issue ERC-1155 tokens or W3C Verifiable Credentials for recovered commodity fractions (copper, aluminum, refined gold) to enable circular manufacturing procurement with verifiable recycled content.

Student Builder Commentary & Hackathon Reflection

Our central engineering objective was resisting the temptation of Web3 speculation. Rather than minting tokens or claiming that distributed ledgers magically eliminate physical fraud, we constrained LoopProof to what cryptography can honestly guarantee: evidence-file integrity checking, rigorous separation of duties, and mathematical mass-balance conservation. This discipline produced a resilient prototype that directly addresses greenwashing in e-waste supply chains.
